Checking our work

When we say 60%, does it happen?

A percentage is only worth something if it comes true about as often as it says. So we check every one we've given: the home win, the draw and the away win in every game. If our 60% calls come off about 6 times in 10, you can take our numbers at face value.

The score beside this grades the percentages themselves, not just the picks: it is the ranked probability score, where lower is better and the gaps that matter are in the third decimal place.

The bookmakers' numbers are their average odds as collected by football-data.co.uk a day or two before each round, with their margin removed by the power method. We publish on Thursday, so they often have more team news than we do. Odds are a benchmark, not betting advice.

Our public test: do we see through luck?

Our ratings lean on the quality of chances rather than the goals that went in. Looking back over past seasons, that seemed to help most when one side's results had run well ahead of, or behind, its chances. It wasn't enough to prove anything, so we've written the test down in advance and will score it on our live calls only.

The rule: take each side's goals minus expected goals, scored and conceded, per game over its last 20 league games. If the two sides differ by {{ '%.2f'|format(prereg.threshold) }} goals a game or more, the match is in the test. Every published call records this number before kick-off. At the end of the season we compare our percentages in those games with the bookmakers'. The full rule is on GitHub, dated by its commit.
