What this is

Every Premier League match, called and explained

Inkwell & Algorithm puts the back pages and the spreadsheet side by side. Every week a statistical model calls each Premier League match as a home win, a draw or an away win, gives the most likely score, and sets out the reasons in plain English with the numbers behind them.

We care more about why than about the percentage. Each call is broken down into the handful of things that moved it, such as one side's attack, the other's defence or a missing striker, and those steps always add up to our number. We check every call against the bookmakers and publish our record, good weeks and bad. It is analysis, not betting advice.

How it works

  1. 1

    Collect

    Results, expected goals, bookmakers' odds and team news for this season and the two before it. Nothing older, because squads and managers change too much.

  2. 2

    Rate

    Every team gets an attack and a defence rating. Recent games count most, and the ratings blend real goals with expected goals (xG), which measures the quality of the chances a team makes and gives up.

  3. 3

    Call

    The two teams' ratings give each side's expected goals, which become win, draw and loss chances and the single most likely score. When neither side is a clear favourite we say it is too close to call.

  4. 4

    Explain

    We break each call into the factors that moved it the most, show it next to the bookmakers' view, and say what would change our mind. After the weekend we mark every call right or wrong.

Where the data comes from

All free, public sources, refreshed every Thursday and Saturday.

SourceWhat we take from it
football-data.co.ukEvery result, with shots, corners and cards, plus the bookmakers' odds we compare ourselves with, for the last three seasons.
UnderstatExpected goals (xG) for both sides of every match.
Fantasy Premier LeagueTeam news: injuries, suspensions and doubts, and which players a side relies on most.
API-FootballThe coming fixtures and kick-off times, pre-match odds and the club crests.

Club crests belong to their clubs. The code behind the site is open on GitHub.
